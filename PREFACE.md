# Preface

I did not create these notes. I created a framework for creating them: meta work, and I have a soft spot for anything meta. A horde of robots helped. They ran OCR jobs, compiled TeX and performed assorted other computational rituals on IT infrastructure scattered around my home and somewhere out in the cloud. Along the way I probably evaporated an impressive quantity of water and did my part to make your RAM and SSDs even more expensive.

It took an entire day. A magnificent exercise in procrastination, given that I could simply have spent that day writing the notes and never worried about what became of them.

The trouble is that, despite having no formal engineering education, I have worked as an engineer for well over a decade. And few things pain an engineer more than producing a useless artifact.

So this is an experiment.

I don't particularly like the textbook in question; I find it thoroughly unengaging. Still, I would rather optimize the exercise than merely endure it.

First, I wanted a guide: a structured list of topics and terms with exact page references. The robots did a reasonably good job of crunching through the material. That alone would have rescued me when I first tried to take notes on electrostatics and got thoroughly lost in the textbook's prose.

Second, I wanted scaffolding for lecture notes that might actually be useful: notes that serve as a reference, support further study and perhaps even outlive the exam they were written for.

A note on academic integrity: nothing here does the assignment for anyone. The wiki and the book contain no finished notes, only scaffolds: what to look up, where to find it and the exact terms the textbook uses. The reading, the thinking and the handwriting remain the student's job, and that is the point. What I am sharing is a vision of how such work could, in theory, be done more efficiently. As future aerospace engineers, we have a lot of physics on our plates, and every hour lost in the prose is an hour not spent understanding the physics.

In short, if I am going to spend time producing an artifact, I would rather produce one worth keeping.
