# 10–12. Поле зарядженої площини · двох площин · сферичної поверхні — каркас

## Recipe box (our method, not from the book; write it once, at the top)
1. Use the symmetry to find the **direction** of E.
2. Choose a closed **Gaussian surface** on which E is constant or the flux is 0.
3. Compute the flux by definition (E·area) and set it equal to $q_{\text{inside}}/\varepsilon_0$.
4. Solve for E. Then get φ from $\varphi_1-\varphi_2 = \int E\,dl$.

## 10. Поле рівномірно зарядженої нескінченної площини (pp. 38–39, 54)
- "Infinite" means the distance to the plane ≪ its size.
- Symmetry: E ⟂ the plane. Surface: a **прямий циліндр** (right cylinder) crossing the plane, with bases S.
- Flux: through the side = 0 and through the two bases = 2ES. Enclosed charge σS.
- $2ES = \dfrac{\sigma S}{\varepsilon_0}$ ⟹ $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$ (1.34)
- E doesn't depend on distance, so the field on each side is **однорідне** (uniform).
- Potential: $\varphi(x) = \varphi(0) - \dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}x$, decreasing linearly.
- ✏️ Sketch рис. 1.18: the plane, a cylinder through it, and arrows out of both bases.

## 11. Поле двох різнойменно заряджених паралельних площин (p. 39)
- +σ and −σ. By **superposition**, the fields add between the planes and cancel outside.
- Between: $E = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$ (1.35). Outside: E = 0.
- Two planes with the **same** sign: E = 0 between them.
- Potential between: $\varphi(x) = \varphi(0) - \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}x$ (p. 55).
- ✏️ Sketch рис. 1.19: two plates, three regions, the field arrows from each plate, and their sum.
- Related: the field right next to a conductor is $E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0$ (1.36), p. 40 ([topic 14](14_Провідник_у_зовнішньому_полі.md)).

## 12. Поле рівномірно зарядженої сферичної поверхні (кулі) (pp. 40, 55)
- Symmetry: E is radial. Surface: a **concentric sphere** of radius r.
- Outside (r > R): $E\cdot4\pi r^2 = q/\varepsilon_0$ ⟹ $E = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r^2}$, the same as a point charge at the centre.
- Inside a **сфера** (conducting ball, charge on the surface): E = 0 and φ = const = $\dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R}$.
- Potential outside: $\varphi(r) = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$.
- Optional, a ball charged through its **volume** with ρ: inside, $E = \dfrac{\rho r}{3\varepsilon_0\varepsilon}$, which grows linearly.
- ✏️ Plot E(r) and φ(r) for the shell: E jumps up at R then falls as 1/r²; φ is flat inside then falls as 1/r.

## Bonus row (book p. 40, cylinder)
- An infinite charged cylinder or thread with linear density τ: $E = \dfrac{\tau}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$ (1.37) outside, 0 inside a cylindrical surface.
  - ⚠️ **Misprint in the book** (p. 40): (1.37) is printed as E = η/(4πε₀r), but the line above it, E·2πrh = ηh/ε₀, gives **η/(2πε₀r)**. The book itself uses η/(2πε₀r) for the cylindrical capacitor (p. 62). The book writes the linear density as **η** here, not τ.
