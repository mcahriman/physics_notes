## [A] Постановка задачі (p. 320)
- The speed is continuous, so the probability of an *exact* speed is 0. The right question: «скільки з усіх наявних молекул мають швидкості, значення яких лежать у певному інтервалі швидкостей в околі заданої швидкості?»
- **Простір швидкостей** (velocity space): each molecule is a point (v_x, v_y, v_z), рис. 14.11.

## [B] Функція розподілу (pp. 322–323)
- $dW = \dfrac{dn}{n} = f(\vec v)\,d\omega$ (14.42). f is the **функція розподілу молекул за швидкостями**, also called the **густина ймовірності** (probability density).
- **Умова нормування**: $\int f\,d\omega = 1$ (14.43): the molecule certainly has *some* speed.
- Directions are equally likely, so f depends only on |v|. The components are independent, so $f = f(v_x)f(v_y)f(v_z)$ (14.46). Maxwell (1859).
- Skip the derivation (pp. 323–327); just note the idea: "independence + isotropy ⟹ Gaussian".

## [C] Закон Максвелла (p. 327) — put it in a box
$$\frac{dn}{n} = \frac{4}{\sqrt\pi}\left(\frac{m_0}{2kT}\right)^{3/2}e^{-\frac{m_0v^2}{2kT}}v^2\,dv\qquad(14.57)$$
- Words: the **relative number of molecules** whose speeds lie between v and v + dv.
- Per component: $f(v_x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt\pi\,v_н}e^{-v_x^2/v_н^2}$ (14.54), a bell curve centred on 0 (рис. 14.14).
- Shape of f(v) (pp. 327–328): f = 0 at v = 0 (no molecules at rest) and f → 0 as v → ∞. It is **asymmetric**: a steep rise and a slow fall. The area under it is 1.
- **Heating** (p. 328): the peak shifts right (∝ √T) and gets lower and wider, because the area stays 1.
- ✏️ The main drawing: two curves for T₁ < T₂, the same area, with v_н, ⟨v⟩ and v_кв marked on one of them. Shade one strip dv as dn/n.
- An example from the book (Table 3, O₂ at 0 °C): about ¾ of the molecules move at 200–600 м/с.

## [D] Характерні швидкості (pp. 328–330)
| швидкість | формула | № |
|---|---|---|
| **найбільш імовірна** v_н (peak of f) | $\sqrt{2kT/m_0} = \sqrt{2RT/M}$ | (14.58) |
| **середня (арифметична)** ⟨v⟩ | $\sqrt{8kT/(\pi m_0)}$ | (14.61) |
| **середня квадратична** v_кв | $\sqrt{3kT/m_0}$ | (14.62) |
- $v_{кв} : \langle v\rangle : v_н \approx 1{,}2 : 1{,}1 : 1$
- Universal form with u = v/v_н: $\dfrac{dn}{n\,du} = \dfrac4{\sqrt\pi}e^{-u^2}u^2$ (14.63). It doesn't depend on the gas or on T.
- Scale (p. 318): H₂ ≈ 1,9 км/с, N₂ ≈ 493 м/с, O₂ ≈ 461 м/с at room temperature.

## [E] Дослідна перевірка (pp. 318–320, 331)
- **Stern's experiment (1920)**: a silver-coated wire inside two rotating cylinders. The deposited strip shifts and **smears**, which shows the atoms have a spread of speeds with a peak. ✏️ Sketch рис. 14.9 from above.
- **Lammert (1929)**: two rotating discs with slits offset by an angle φ act as a speed selector. It confirmed Maxwell's law with high accuracy.

## Self-check
1. Why is the probability of an exact speed zero?
2. Why does the peak get lower when the gas is heated?
3. Order v_н, ⟨v⟩ and v_кв from smallest to largest, and explain why.

## Addendum (not in the book)
Our shorthand and derivations. The book doesn't print these forms.
- **Names of the three speeds** (→ [D]): the book's $v_н$ (найбільш імовірна) is the English most probable speed $v_p$; ⟨v⟩ is the mean speed $\bar v$; $v_{кв}$, often printed as $\langle v_{кв}\rangle$, is the rms speed $v_{rms}$. The book also calls $v_{кв}$ the **теплова швидкість** (thermal speed, p. 318).
